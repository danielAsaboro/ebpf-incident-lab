# Learning evaluation protocol

## Purpose

Determine whether Linux practitioners can select discriminating evidence, interpret it, bound a conclusion, and propose a useful next measurement without the guide. Platform completion, tutor approval, elapsed time, and satisfaction are not proof of independent learning.

## Formative pilot

Recruit 8–12 consenting Linux practitioners new to eBPF investigation. Collect prerequisite performance and relevant background without names/employers in analysis records. Use random participant identifiers; keep any contact mapping in a separate private location. Inform participants exactly which reasoning or evidence may reach an AI provider; an authored-only path remains available. Do not commit participant records, recordings, transcripts, or raw observations to this repository.

Have each learner complete the Lab 02 baseline, explain selected evidence, and attempt a changed fixed scenario. Observe usability and misconceptions. Return after seven days for the alternative recall problem. Record first response, assistance, revisions, task timing, and whether the learner used outside materials. No surveillance or answer-copying detector is assumed. Early recall is labelled practice; late recall retains actual timing.

## Rubric

Score each dimension 0 (missing or contradicted), 1 (partly supported), 2 (supported and bounded):

1. Claim: a defensible conclusion within the observation boundary.
2. Evidence: specific records and their bearing on competing explanations.
3. Mechanism: correct relationship between hook, correlation, and result.
4. Uncertainty: an alternative or fact not settled by these observations.
5. Next experiment: a discriminating measurement with interpretable possible outcomes.

Two reviewers independently score unfamiliar-task responses without viewing instructional condition, hint history, or later revisions first. Record disagreements and adjudication explicitly. Reviewers may mark the case unscorable if execution or evidence is inadequate. Do not silently replace missing data with zero. Preserve baseline and delayed scores separately; report assistance and attrition.

## Tutor evaluation

Use expert-authored examples covering terse correct reasoning, articulate misconceptions, partially correct answers, unsupported causal claims, absent evidence, malformed evidence, and answer-seeking prompts. Record the exact curriculum/model version, input, output, reviewer judgment, and response latency in private files. Track factual errors, unsupported praise, answer leakage, and usefulness of the follow-up. Tutor self-evaluation is not a gold standard.

## Comparison before efficacy claims

Use pilot results to design a larger comparison against the article-plus-lab experience. Match prerequisite knowledge; randomize instructional condition where feasible. Use distinct but comparable unfamiliar assessments and blinded scoring, with delayed retention. Predefine the analysis, handling of missing outcomes, and power/sample-size rationale before collecting comparison outcomes. Distinguish treatment effects from differences in instruction time, prior expertise, and outside help.

Publish only consented aggregate findings and their limitations. RareSkills learning-speed reports remain external claims, not eBPF outcomes or promised results.

## Private record handling

Obtain consent before collection, define a study-specific retention date, provide a withdrawal/deletion contact, and restrict raw access to the research team. The local app itself has no central participant database. Export only when needed; do not paste raw records into issues, public reports, or fellowship applications. Establish the private study store and its access controls before recruitment.
